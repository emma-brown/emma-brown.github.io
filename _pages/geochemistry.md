        <h2>Hydrothermal Decomposition of Model Organic Acids</h2>

        <p>
        This project is part of a broader effort to understand how organic
        molecules behave in water. A lot of organic chemical reactions occur on
        the Earth or within a cell <em>in water</em>. Therefore, our research
        lab uses water as the solvent instead of more traditional organic
        solvents in our experiments. Specifically, I investigate the
        decomposition of model organic acids and their sodium salts across a
        range of temperatures under hydrothermal conditions. By incorporating
        dissolved salts and mineral phases into laboratory experiments, my work
        aims to better replicate natural systems and determine how these
        components influence the reactivity and stability of organic acids.
        </p>

        <h2>Thermodynamic Properties of Aqueous Aromatic Compounds</h2>

        <p>
        Current thermodynamic models of organic compounds in natural systems are
        limited by available data. There has been great progress in measuring
        and estimating thermodynamic properties of aliphatic organic compounds
        in the aqueous state; however, comparable thermodynamic properties for
        aromatic compounds, especially polyfunctional aromatics, remain scarce.
        To expand current thermodynamic databases, we gathered or estimated
        standard partial molar thermodynamic properties of twelve aromatic
        carboxylic acids and their fourteen anions. Stability constants and
        thermodynamic data for their metal complexes were also gathered or
        estimated and incorporated into the study. This expanded database
        enables modeling of aromatic carboxylic and polyfunctional acids and
        their metal complexes in natural systems from 0–350 °C and 1–5
        kilobars.
        </p>
