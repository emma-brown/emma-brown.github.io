## Hydrothermal decomposition of model organic acids in salty solutions

This project is part of a broader effort to understand how organic molecules behave in water. I investigate the decomposition of model organic acids and their sodium salts across a range of temperatures under hydrothermal conditions. By incorporating dissolved salts and mineral phases into laboratory experiments, my work aims to better replicate natural systems and determine how these components influence the reactivity and stability of organic acids. 

## Thermodynamic properties of aqueous aromatic acids and their metal complexes across temperature and pressure

Current thermodynamic models of organic compounds in natural systems are limited by what data is available. There has been great progress in measuring and estimating thermodynamic properties of organic compounds in the aqueous state, especially for aliphatic compounds. However thermodynamic properties for aromatic compounds, especially polyfunctional aromatics, in the aqueous state are still lacking in comparison. To expand current thermodynamic databases, we gathered or estimated standard partial molar thermodynamic properties of 12 aromatic carboxylic acids and their 14 anions. Stability constants and other thermodynamic measurements for the metal complexes of the aromatic species were also gathered or estimated and incorporated into the study. This addition to thermodynamic databases will enable the modeling of aromatic carboxylic and polyfunctional acids and their metal complexes in natural systems from 0-350°C and 1-5 kilobars.
